The problem

Good media buying, reported badly, looks like bad media buying.

Your client decides whether your work is paying off by reading a report. In most agencies, that report is the weakest part of the service. It usually breaks in one of four places.

01

Hours lost to manual reporting

Every week, someone on your team opens Ads Manager, the CRM and Stripe in three tabs and copies numbers into a spreadsheet or a slide deck. It takes hours. Mistakes slip in. Next week, it starts again. That time should go into the accounts, not into the paperwork about the accounts.

02

Numbers that don't match

Meta reports one lead count. The CRM reports another. Your client notices, and the next call is spent explaining the gap instead of the results. Even when the campaign is working, the conversation is about whose number is right.

03

Clients who can't see the results

Your client wants to know one thing: did the money come back? If they can't follow the line from ad spend to booked calls to sales to cash in the bank, they start to doubt the work. Doubt is how retainers end, often with no warning.

04

No view of profit

Most reporting stops at cost per lead and ROAS. Those numbers matter, but they don't answer the owner's real question. Nobody can show what the ads actually returned once cash collected and costs are counted.

What it costs

Weak reporting costs more than the hours it takes.

The time spent building reports is the cost you can see. The larger costs are harder to spot until they arrive.

Lost time

Senior people end up doing data entry. Every hour spent copying numbers is an hour not spent on creative, testing or a new client. As the client list grows, the reporting work grows with it.

Lost trust

When the numbers don't line up, the client stops trusting all of them, including the good ones. Your best results get questioned because the report around them looks unreliable.

Lost retainers

Clients rarely leave because of one bad week. They leave because they could never clearly see what they were paying for. A client who can see the return has a reason to stay. A client who can't is waiting for a reason to go.

The new way

A reporting department, without hiring one.

You keep doing what you were hired for: running the ads. I take over the reporting layer. That means connecting each client's platforms, building the dashboards, checking the data and keeping everything running as platforms change.

Your client gets a single place to see spend, leads, booked calls, sales and cash collected, with the same numbers every time they look. You get your hours back and a report that supports your work instead of undermining it.

Why don't the numbers in Ads Manager and the CRM match? Can you fix that?

There are usually a few reasons. Meta credits a lead to an ad based on its attribution window, which can include people who saw an ad days earlier. The same person may submit a form twice and appear once in the CRM. Some CRM leads come from organic sources, not ads. And a broken form or integration can stop a lead from reaching the CRM at all.

I reconcile these sources and show both numbers side by side, with the reason for the gap. The two figures won't always match exactly, and I won't pretend they do. What changes is that you and your client can see why they differ.
